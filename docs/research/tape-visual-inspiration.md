# Tape visual inspiration

Research date: 2026-10-26.

The brief is two exposed reels, moving tape, and a playhead inside a square display. The layout suggestions below are design interpretations, not claims that these machines use those exact layouts. Keep the existing monochrome pixel treatment and use original drawing code rather than copying product imagery.

## 1. Nagra IV-S: compact transport

[Nagra's IV-S product page](https://www.nagraaudio.com/product/nagra-iv-s/) supplies an official product photograph and manuals. Its [IV-S data sheet](https://www.nagraaudio.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/NAGRA_IV-S1.pdf) identifies separate recording and playback heads. The [official product photograph](https://www.nagraaudio.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/1971-IV-S-Version-IV-2-1920x1080.jpg) is a useful reference for the exposed transport.

Try a triangle in the square: reels across the upper half, tape descending toward a head near the lower center. Give the two downward tape spans enough length for slow wow and faster flutter to read. The playhead should sit directly on the visible path, with the tape brighter than the reel bodies.

## 2. Revox B77: balanced reel geometry

[Revox's B77 product page](https://revox.com/en-de/products/b77) supplies front and detail photographs, a brochure, and operating instructions. The page identifies the tape heads, pinch roller, and capstan as separate parts of the transport. A [direct official product image](https://revox.com/cdn/shop/files/Revox_B77_MKIII_Stereo_Tape_Recorder_00.avif?v=1785397632&width=1920) provides the overall reference.

Try a wide V: two large reels near the upper corners, guides toward the lower corners, and an exposed tape span across a small central head. Fill most of the square with the transport. Avoid shrinking the entire machine into a landscape strip. Thin reel spokes and quiet outer rings leave contrast for the moving ribbon.

## 3. Roland Space Echo: tape as the subject

[BOSS's history of the Space Echo](https://articles.boss.info/history-of-the-space-echo/) describes a long tape loop that spools freely into a chamber, plus a record head and three playback heads. Roland's [original RE-201 manual](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/RE-201_OM.pdf) provides first-party transport documentation. The RE-201's free loop is a different mechanism from two reel transport, so borrow its emphasis on the tape rather than presenting the result as an RE-201 diagram.

Try a diagonal composition: one reel upper left, the other lower right, and a long exposed tape path crossing the center. Place the head on the central crossing. This uses the square's height and gives movement more room. Animate texture traveling along the tape, then layer small path displacement onto it. Echo can appear as faint trailing marks downstream of the head.

## Candidate priority

The triangle is the strongest first candidate because it leaves a large, readable tape path while retaining familiar two-reel symmetry. The diagonal is the clearest alternative for using the whole square. Compare both against the wide V with effects turned down and up, so the chosen composition works before and during visible modulation.
